An AI-powered appointment booking assistant that takes a form submission (from Jotform) and fully automates checking availability, booking the calendar, logging records, and emailing the customer, with no human touching it unless something goes wrong.

How it works, step by step
  • 1. Trigger (Webhook): Jotform posts form submissions to this webhook, using Jotform's auto-generated plain-English summary of all form answers.
  • 2. Extract Appointment Details (Gemini AI): An AI “Information Extractor” reads that raw text and pulls out structured fields (name, email, address, phone, date and time), auto-converting natural language like “2pm” into 24-hour format.
  • 3. Validate Required Fields: Checks that name, a valid email, and a date were actually extracted. If not, the customer gets an immediate error asking them to resubmit: no bad data enters the system.
  • 4. Prepare Appointment Data: Builds a clean record with a 1-hour time slot and marks status as “Pending.”
  • 5. Create Airtable Record (Pending): Logs the request immediately, so there's a record even before availability is confirmed.
  • 6. Check Google Calendar availability: Pulls all events for that business day (9am-5pm) and checks for overlaps with the requested slot.
  • 7. Branch: Is the slot available? Yes → creates the calendar event, logs to Sheets, emails a confirmation, returns 200. No → marks “Needs Reschedule,” emails up to 3 alternative times, returns 200 needs_reschedule.
  • 8. Error handling: Every external step has a fallback: failures email the admin directly and return a 500, so nothing fails silently.
Business value

  • No double-booking: actively checks the calendar before confirming
  • Full audit trail: every request lands in Airtable, successful bookings also land in a Google Sheet
  • Graceful degradation: bad input is rejected politely; system failures alert the admin directly instead of losing the customer's request

Automated Sales Call Tracker & Dashboard

ProblemSales calls were logged by hand, so show rates and close rates were always out of date.
What I builtAbout 10 small n8n workflows: Calendly booking, Google Meet recording, Gemini transcript and call outcome, a notes doc and a live dashboard.
ResultEvery sales call is logged, summarised and counted with zero manual entry. Calls with no transcript after 24 hours get flagged instead of forgotten.
⌄

A production automation built for a real client (Willers Coaching): an automated pipeline that turns every sales call into structured data with zero manual entry, and shows it on a live dashboard. Built entirely in n8n, wired to Calendly, Google Sheets/Drive/Meet, and Gemini AI. It solves a simple but common problem: coaches book discovery calls, run them, and rarely log consistently afterward (did they show up, did they buy, what's next?), so there's no reliable view of booking volume, show rate, or close rate. This system logs all of that automatically from the call itself.

How it works, end to end
  • Booking: A prospect books a call in Calendly. Calendly fires a signed webhook to n8n, which creates a row in the “Bookings” Google Sheet: name, email, event type, scheduled time, status Booked.
  • The call: Runs on Google Meet, which saves the recording to a Drive folder automatically.
  • Transcription: Every 10 minutes, n8n checks that folder. When it finds a new recording, it sends the video to Gemini, which transcribes the full conversation.
  • Analysis: The transcript is matched back to the right booking row (by name + time), then Gemini classifies two things: attendance (Yes / No / Unverified, where Unverified means the transcript was too thin to tell rather than being guessed as a no-show), and outcome (Closed / Not Closed / Follow-up Required / Manual Review).
  • Notes document: A formatted Google Doc is generated (summary, next steps, key details and the full transcript), with a link added to the sheet row.
  • Dashboard: The row updates and the dashboard rebuilds automatically, so booked/attended/closed counts, show rate, close rate, and weekly/monthly breakdowns are always current.
Supporting pieces
  • No-show sync: If Calendly marks a no-show, that flows to the row automatically.
  • Reschedule handling: A moved call keeps both rows (original marked Rescheduled, new one linked to it) so history stays visible, but it's only counted once.
  • Grace Period Check: A safety net: any call that came and went with no transcript after 24 hours gets flagged Manual Review instead of sitting forgotten.
  • Manual controls: Two small endpoints (or direct sheet editing) let a human override attendance or outcome for anything the AI couldn't resolve.
  • Error handler: Any workflow failure logs to a sheet and emails the client directly.
  • Dashboard access: A single key-gated URL that opens in any browser and reads live from the sheet.
Architecture note

This isn't one large workflow: it's roughly 10 small, single-purpose workflows (Calendly Booking Sync, Interview Pipeline, Grace Period Check, Rebuild Dashboard, the two APIs, and others), all reading and writing the same Google Sheet as the single source of truth.

n8n · Customer Support · AI Triage

Support Inbox → AI Triage

ProblemEvery support email had to be read, sorted and answered from scratch.
What I builtThree n8n workflows: Gemini sorts each email into 1 of 6 categories and drafts a reply; a person approves it in Airtable; flagged emails go to Slack.
ResultA ready draft waits for every email, and the AI never sends anything without a person approving it.
⌄

A three-workflow n8n system that reads incoming support email, classifies and drafts a reply with Gemini, and only ever sends after a human approves. Built and validated end-to-end: 3 workflows, 31 total nodes, Gemini 2.5 Flash, 0 errors / 0 warnings on validation, ~2 hours build time. Currently inactive pending go-live.

What I built: three focused workflows
Gmail Trigger→ Classify (Gemini)→ Draft reply (Gemini)→ Log to Airtable→ Slack if flagged
Every 1 min→ Find “Approved” rows→ Create Gmail draft→ Mark sent / failed
Error Trigger→ Slack: workflow crashed
How it works, start to finish
  • 1. Gmail Trigger fires on new inbox mail: a standard polling trigger, no webhook to expose or maintain.
  • 2. Prompt Config stamps every editable setting onto the item: which two Gemini models to use, the 0.8 confidence threshold, the company name/signature, and both full system prompts. Nothing below this node has a hard-coded prompt: everything reads from here.
  • 3. Normalize Message strips quoted reply chains, collapses whitespace, and caps the body at 8,000 characters before it reaches a model, also extracts a clean message ID, thread ID, sender info, and subject.
  • 4. Skip Already-Processed Emails de-duplicates on message ID against the last 10,000 items seen, across executions, not just within one run.
  • 5. Process One at a Time (batch size 1) isolates each email so one bad message can't take the rest of the batch down, and keeps Gemini calls well under rate limits.
  • 6. Create Log Record upserts an Airtable row at Status = Processing before any AI call runs: this is what makes crash-recovery work.
  • 7. Classify Request: Gemini 2.5 Flash at temperature 0, constrained by a structured-output schema (with auto-fix) to exactly one of 6 categories, a 0-1 confidence score, a boolean needs_human_review, a reason, and a one-line summary.
  • 8. Draft Reply: Gemini 2.5 Flash at temperature 0.4, writes the reply using the classification, the original email, and the editable signature.
  • 9. Evaluate Flags computes why (if at all) this needs a human: confidence below threshold, category is Refund Request, or the model's own needs_human_review.
  • 10. Save Triage Result writes category, confidence, summary, draft, and status back onto the same Airtable row.
  • 11. Branch on Needs Human Review?: flagged rows post to #support-triage in Slack with a deep link straight to the Airtable record; clean rows just loop to the next email.
  • 12. Separately, once a person sets a row's status to Approved in Airtable, a scheduled workflow (polling every minute) creates a real Gmail draft (it never sends) and flips the row to “Draft in Gmail” so it can't be drafted twice.
  • 13. Independent of all of the above, a dedicated error-alert workflow sits on an Error Trigger and pings Slack with the workflow name, failed node, error, and execution link if the main workflow ever crashes outright.
Why I structured it this way
  • Human-in-the-loop, not human-on-the-loop: the AI never sends anything. It proposes a category and a draft; a person approves in Airtable; only then does a real Gmail draft get created, and even that still needs a human to hit send.
  • Three workflows, three triggers: triage (event), drafting (schedule), and crash-alerting (failure) are different concerns on different clocks. Each can be tested, paused, or changed without touching the others.
  • One place to edit the AI: every model name, threshold, and prompt lives in a single Prompt Config node: a non-engineer can retune tone or swap models without opening an expression.
  • Structured output over free text: the classifier is contractually forced into 6 known categories with a numeric confidence, so routing logic reads real fields instead of parsing prose.
  • Two temperatures, two jobs: 0 for classification: repeatable, boring, correct. 0.4 for drafting: natural-sounding without wandering off the brand voice.
  • Airtable as the shared spine: one Status field that all three workflows read and write is the entire coordination mechanism: no queue, no extra database, easy for a human to audit by eye.
Edge cases & error handling
  • Duplicate / re-triggered emails: cross-execution dedup on message ID, plus the log write is an upsert keyed on the same field, so a re-run can't create a second row.
  • Low-confidence or ambiguous classification: anything under the 0.8 threshold routes to Needs Review regardless of category.
  • Refund requests: always routed to Needs Review, even at high confidence, because money leaving the business gets a standing human check.
  • Angry, legal, security, or churn-risk emails: the classifier prompt itself lists these as explicit needs_human_review triggers.
  • Long emails / quoted thread history: body is stripped of quoted replies and capped at 8,000 characters before it reaches the model.
  • Missing sender name: falls back to “unknown” rather than breaking the prompt template.
  • A single email fails AI or Airtable: retried 3× with backoff, then isolated: that one row is marked Error with the real error text and Slacked, while the batch-of-1 loop keeps going.
  • The whole workflow crashes (bad credential, n8n restart mid-run): the separate Error Trigger workflow still fires and pings Slack, catching what the per-item handling above can't.
  • Double-drafting an approved reply: the draft-sender flips status to “Draft in Gmail” the moment it succeeds, so the next minute's poll skips it.
What I'd improve before production
  • Swap the placeholder identity for the real client's name and signature: a one-field change in Prompt Config.
  • Calibrate the 0.8 confidence threshold and the 6-category taxonomy against a batch of real historical tickets instead of intuition.
  • Build a small labeled eval set for the classifier so future prompt or model changes are regression-tested, not eyeballed.
  • Make replies thread-aware: a reply on an existing thread currently opens a brand-new logged request instead of linking back via thread ID.
  • Move the draft-sender off a 1-minute poll onto an Airtable-automation webhook, so approved replies go out the moment they're approved.
  • Add a fallback model or provider for the two Gemini calls so a provider outage doesn't stall the inbox.
  • At real ticket volume, weigh Airtable-as-system-of-record against a dedicated helpdesk tool for SLAs and multi-agent assignment.

What I builtSix n8n workflows: find businesses on OpenStreetMap, have AI audit each website, save good leads to Google Sheets, Slack and HubSpot, then queue, send, track and follow up a personal email.
ResultLive and running on its own every weekday since October 2026. One test run found 81 dentists, audited 10 sites, saved 10 leads (6 with an email) and created or updated 10 HubSpot companies.
⌄

A lead engine I run for my own business. It picks the next industry and Texas city, finds businesses that have a website, reads each site, and has an AI score how much help it needs (missing online booking, no chat, no lead capture). Strong leads land in a Google Sheet, Slack and HubSpot, and an outreach email that names the real gap on their site is written, safety-checked and queued. Separate workflows send the queue in Texas business hours, read the replies, and send one follow-up in the same thread.

How the system fits together
Weekdays, hourly→ Pick next industry + city→ OpenStreetMap search→ Skip chains + already audited
Fetch website→ Detect booking / chat / forms→ AI audit + score→ Score ≥ 8 only
Find contact email→ Google Sheet + Slack→ HubSpot company→ AI writes outreach→ Safety check → queue
Texas 9:30am–3:30pm→ Do-not-contact + MX check→ Send (10/day)→ HubSpot: Attempted to contact
Reply arrives→ AI classifies reply→ Do-not-contact / HubSpot status / audit draft
Error Trigger→ Slack alert
How it works, start to finish
  • 1. Pick the next search. An hourly weekday schedule rotates through 10 industries across Austin, Dallas and San Antonio, and stops for the day once it has 10 leads that have an email address.
  • 2. Find businesses for free. OpenStreetMap (Nominatim for the city, Overpass for businesses) instead of a paid lead database, with a mirror server as a fallback because the free servers are sometimes down.
  • 3. Skip what is not worth auditing: chains and franchises, duplicate domains, and anything audited on an earlier day.
  • 4. Read the website. A Code node pulls signals from the HTML: booking tools (by vendor), chat widgets (by script domain), real forms, and same-site booking pages.
  • 5. AI audit. A Groq model returns a structured score and the single biggest gap, with a strict scoring scale so it does not rate every site 9–10.
  • 6. Save the lead to Google Sheets (no duplicates), post it to Slack, and create or update the company in HubSpot with Lead Status New.
  • 7. Write the outreach. The AI writes a short email about that site’s real gap; a safety check blocks anything that breaks the rules before it is queued. Leads with no email get a WhatsApp message and click-to-chat link in Slack instead.
  • 8. Send, read, follow up. The sender works through the queue in Texas business hours; the reply handler classifies each reply (interested, question, not interested, auto-reply) and updates HubSpot; one follow-up goes out after 4 days, in the same Gmail thread.
Edge cases & error handling
  • Never email the wrong people: a do-not-contact list (opt-outs and bounces are added automatically), an MX check on every domain, and a hard daily send limit.
  • AI misread a site: the first real batch showed the audit missed booking forms loaded by JavaScript on 4 of 6 sites. I added vendor detection and “verified facts” that override the AI before any email is written.
  • Bot-protection pages (Cloudflare “Just a moment”) are marked as blocked, not as “site down”.
  • Rate limits: AI calls run one at a time with a delay to stay inside the free tier; HubSpot searches are throttled after a 429 in testing.
  • Missed runs: n8n runs on my PC, so schedules repeat hourly with a once-per-day guard and only mark a search done after it succeeds.
  • Leads with no way to contact them are removed automatically after 23 hours.
  • Any crash triggers a separate Error Trigger workflow that posts the failed node and error to Slack.

AI Chat Assistant for This Website

ProblemVisitors have questions at any hour, and a contact form alone loses the people who are not ready to fill it in.
What I builtThe chat in the corner of this page. An n8n workflow answers from a fixed set of business facts with an AI model; a second workflow sends chat leads to Slack and HubSpot.
ResultLive on this site since October 2026. Try the chat button in the bottom-right corner.
⌄

A website assistant that answers questions about my services, never invents prices, asks one follow-up question, and offers the free audit. Answers come only from a fixed list of business facts written into the prompt. Because n8n runs on my own PC, a Cloudflare Worker sits in front: it keeps the chat answering 24/7 and holds leads in a queue when n8n is offline, retrying every 15 minutes.

Two small workflows
Visitor message→ Validate + trim history→ AI answer (business facts only)→ Reply to the chat
Visitor leaves details→ Looks real?→ Slack→ HubSpot contact
n8n offline→ Worker queues the lead→ Retry every 15 min
Why I built it this way
  • Facts, not guesses: the assistant may only use the business facts in its prompt, so it cannot make up prices, clients or services.
  • Clear failure answers: a bad request and an AI error each get their own response instead of a hanging chat.
  • Spam filter before the CRM: leads are validated before anything reaches Slack or HubSpot.
  • Always on: the Worker answers even when my PC is off, and no lead is lost while n8n is down.
What I’d improve
  • Read the business facts from one shared file at run time instead of copying them into the prompt.
  • Log questions the assistant could not answer, to find gaps in the website.

n8n · Video · AI

Reels Maker: Long Video → Captioned Vertical Reels

ProblemTurning one long video into short vertical clips with captions takes hours of editing for every video.
What I builtDrop a long video into a folder. A script transcribes it, an AI picks the best 30–60 second moments, and ffmpeg renders 1080×1920 reels with word-by-word captions. An n8n workflow posts the result to Slack.
ResultHands-off: the folder watcher starts with my PC, and every finished batch (or failure) shows up in Slack.
⌄

A small production line for short-form video. Whisper transcribes the audio with word timings; a language model picks self-contained moments and writes a hook; a vision model looks at frames to decide whether to crop on a speaker’s face or fit the frame on a blurred background. Captions show 2–3 words at a time with the current word highlighted.

The pipeline
Video dropped in folder→ Whisper transcript (word timings)→ AI picks 30–60s moments
Vision: crop on face or fit→ ffmpeg 1080×1920 + captions→ n8n webhook→ Slack
Why it is split this way
  • Files stay out of n8n: recent n8n versions turn off Execute Command and the local file trigger by default, and my n8n is reachable from the internet, so the file work runs in a local script and n8n only handles the notification.
  • Free models with fallbacks: each AI step goes through my own model gateway, which falls back to another provider if one is down.
  • Success and failure both report: the Slack message says which reels were made, or why a video failed, and the source file is moved to done or failed.
What I’d improve
  • Test the face-crop path on more real talking-head footage.
  • Post approved reels straight to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts.

n8n · WhatsApp · AI Agent · CRM

WhatsApp AI Auto-Reply + Mini CRM

ProblemSmall businesses answer the same WhatsApp questions all day and lose track of who asked what.
What I builtOne n8n workflow: every incoming message updates a contact in a 3-table CRM, an AI agent answers from the business’s FAQ table and saves lead details it learns, and every reply is logged.
ResultBuilt and validated in n8n. Not live yet: it needs a WhatsApp Business account connected to go live.
⌄

An AI receptionist for WhatsApp with its own CRM. The CRM is three n8n Data Tables: Contacts (stage, interest, message count, needs-human flag), Messages (every message in and out) and Business Info (the FAQ the agent answers from). The business edits answers in a table, not in a prompt.

The flow
WhatsApp message→ Text messages only→ Find or create contact→ Log message
AI agent (memory per number)→ Tool: look up FAQ→ Tool: save lead detail→ Reply on WhatsApp→ Log reply
AI or send fails→ Fallback reply + Slack alert
Design choices
  • Answers come from a table: the agent looks up the Business Info table, so changing an answer needs no workflow edit.
  • Remembers each customer: conversation memory is keyed on the WhatsApp number (last 30 messages).
  • Captures leads as it chats: a tool lets the agent save a name, email or interest to the contact row.
  • Never silent: if the AI fails, the customer still gets a polite fallback reply, and the team is alerted in Slack.
What I’d improve before production
  • Make “save lead detail” update only the fields it learned, so a blank value can never overwrite an earlier one.
  • Hand the chat to a person when a contact is flagged “needs human”.

Common Questions

Things people usually wonder before reaching out.

What if I don't know exactly what I need automated?

⌄
That's completely normal, and it's actually where most projects start. On our first call, I'll ask about your day-to-day workflow and point out the repetitive parts that are good automation candidates: you don't need to arrive with a technical spec.

How long does a typical project take?

⌄
It depends on scope, but most single-workflow automations (like lead routing, booking, or invoice follow-up) take about 1-2 weeks from kickoff to a tested, working system. I'll give you a specific timeline as part of the proposal, not a vague estimate.

What if something breaks after you're done?

⌄
Every automation I build includes error alerts, so if something fails, you (and I) find out immediately instead of silently. I stay available after handoff to fix issues or adjust the workflow as your process changes.

Do you work with businesses outside the Philippines?

⌄
Yes, all of my work is remote and platform-based (n8n, Make.com, Zapier all run in the cloud), so location isn't a barrier. I'm happy to adjust working hours to overlap with your team's timezone.

What if I already have an automation that's not working well?

⌄
I can review an existing workflow, find where it's breaking down or missing error handling, and either fix it or rebuild the parts that need it: you don't need to start from zero.

How much does a project cost?

⌄
It depends on the size of the job. After a free 30-minute call I send you a fixed quote, so you know the full cost before any work starts. Small fixes are a one-off fee; bigger systems are a one-time build fee plus an optional monthly care plan.

Will it work with the tools I already use?

⌄
Usually, yes. I've connected Gmail, Google Sheets, Calendar and Drive, HubSpot, HighLevel, Airtable, Slack, Calendly, Jotform and ManyChat. If your tool has an API or webhooks, n8n, Make or Zapier can almost always connect it, and if it can't, I'll tell you on the call.
